
ABSTRACT
Improvised Explosive Devices (IEDs) continue to pose significant threats to military personnel, humanitarian demining operations, and civilian populations due to their concealed nature and the limitations of conventional detection methods. This study presents the design and development of an underground IED detection system using sensor fusion to improve the reliability of buried object detection while minimizing false alarms. The system integrates a 5 × 5 QMC5883L magnetometer array, an AMG8833 8 × 8 infrared thermal sensor, and MQ-135 and MQ-2 gas sensors mounted on a remotely operated rover with an acrylic chassis. A Raspberry Pi 4 serves as the central processing unit for sensor acquisition, feature extraction, feature-level sensor fusion, and machine learning-based classification. The rover is wirelessly controlled using a joystick, while an ESP32-CAM provides live video streaming to a PyQt-based Human–Machine Interface (HMI), which displays magnetic and thermal heatmaps, gas sensor readings, live camera footage, and real-time prediction results. Experimental data were collected directly from the developed prototype by scanning representative buried explosive surrogates comprising aluminum foil, metallic nails, pins, clips, and chemical vapour sources under controlled conditions. Relevant statistical features were extracted from the magnetic, thermal, and gas sensor outputs and combined using a feature-level sensor fusion approach. A Random Forest classifier was trained on the fused dataset to distinguish between target and non-target conditions. The integrated system successfully demonstrated real-time data acquisition, visualization, and classification, showing that combining complementary sensing modalities improves detection reliability compared to the use of individual sensors alone. Although the prototype was successfully implemented, limitations such as the Raspberry Pi's lack of native analogue input, high power consumption, and the slower response characteristics of the gas sensors were identified. Nevertheless, the developed system demonstrates the feasibility of integrating multi-sensor fusion, machine learning, and mobile robotics into a cost-effective platform for intelligent underground explosive detection. The proposed architecture provides a foundation for future enhancements, including autonomous navigation, improved power management, and large-scale field validation.
